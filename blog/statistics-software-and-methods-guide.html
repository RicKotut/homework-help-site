Statistics Methods & Software

The Statistics Software and Methods Guide

A routine that works in any package, and where to go for each tool and method.

Most students looking for data analysis help aren’t stuck on the statistics itself. They’re stuck in the software: the right menu is buried, the output has thirty numbers when you need three, and the assignment wants an interpretation in plain English. This guide gives you a routine that works in any package, plus step-by-step guides to the tools and methods that come up most in college statistics courses.

Two skills, not one

A software assignment tests two things at once. You have to know which analysis fits your question, and you have to know how to run it and read the result in your course’s tool. Either can fail on its own: the right test with the wrong click, or a perfect output you can’t interpret. Most statistical analysis help comes down to finding which of the two is the problem.

A routine for any software assignment

  1. Write the hypotheses and choose the test before you open the software. Our guide to choosing a statistical test shows how.
  2. Check your data. Look at the variable types, how the groups are coded, and whether any values are missing or mistyped.
  3. Run the test with the menu or function your course uses.
  4. Find three numbers: the test statistic, the p-value, and the group means or another measure of effect size.
  5. Write the interpretation in plain language, in the format your course asks for.
  6. Sanity-check the result. If you can, run a tiny sample by hand, or plot the data and see whether the picture matches the numbers.

For thesis-level and graduate work

The routine is the same, but expect more to be asked of you. Graduate programs usually want the assumptions checked and reported, an effect size alongside the p-value, and the results written up in a specific style. Do the software part first, then read your department’s reporting guidelines before you write anything up.
