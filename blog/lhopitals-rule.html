College Math & Calculus

When to Use L’Hôpital’s Rule (and When It Doesn’t Apply)

Part of the College Calculus Survival Guide

L’Hôpital’s Rule helps evaluate certain limits, but only after direct substitution produces 0/0 or ∞/∞. Check the form first; for either of these indeterminate forms, differentiate the numerator and denominator separately, then evaluate the new limit.

When L’Hôpital’s Rule applies

L’Hôpital’s Rule applies when a limit has the indeterminate form 0/0 or ∞/∞. If direct substitution gives a real number, including 0 divided by a nonzero number, evaluate the limit directly; the rule is not needed.

The step-by-step method

  1. Plug in the limit value first to confirm you get 0/0 or ∞/∞. This step is essential.
  2. Take the derivative of the numerator and denominator separately — not the derivative of the whole fraction using the quotient rule.
  3. Evaluate the new limit. If it resolves to a number, you’re done.
  4. If it’s still indeterminate, apply the rule again on the new fraction, and repeat until it resolves.

Worked example

Evaluate the limit as x → 0 of (sin x) / x

Plugging in x = 0 gives 0/0 — an indeterminate form, so L’Hôpital’s Rule applies.

Differentiate top and bottom separately: derivative of sin x is cos x; derivative of x is 1.

New limit: cos(0) / 1 = 1

So the original limit equals 1.

Common mistakes

Applying it to a limit that is not indeterminate. If plugging in gives something like 5/0 (not 0/0), the limit may be infinite or may not exist — L’Hôpital’s Rule does not apply.

Using the quotient rule instead of differentiating top and bottom separately. L’Hôpital’s Rule is not the quotient rule — differentiate the numerator and denominator independently, not the fraction as a whole.
