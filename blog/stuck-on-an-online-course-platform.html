Online Course Platforms

Stuck on an Online Course Platform?

A college student’s guide to online class help, and where to go for each platform.

Most requests for online class help start the same way. It’s late, something is due, and a course platform keeps saying “incorrect” without saying why. The material usually isn’t the real problem. In an online course nobody notices when you fall behind, the software only tells you right or wrong, and a new deadline arrives every week whether or not the last topic made sense.

This guide covers what works for staying on top of an online course, and where to find help with online courses on the platforms students find most frustrating.

Why online courses feel harder than they look

In a classroom, confusion shows on your face and a professor can stop and re-explain. Online, the only feedback is a score. You can sit through a video, feel like you followed it, and discover on the first graded problem that you didn’t. Add randomized homework, limited attempts, and strict formatting rules for answers, and it’s easy to lose points on things that have nothing to do with how well you understand the subject.

Tips for online classes that actually work

  1. Build a learning schedule. Block the same hours every week for the course, as if it met in a room. A schedule beats good intentions.
  2. Set achievable learning goals. “Finish chapter 4 homework by Wednesday” works. “Catch up on the course” doesn’t.
  3. Read the assignment settings first. Check the number of attempts, the time limit, and the deadline before you start, not after you’ve used an attempt.
  4. Start early. Make your first attempt at least two days before the deadline, so you still have time to get help.
  5. Use your online learning resources. Instructor office hours, the campus tutoring center, the eText, and recorded lectures are usually included in your tuition and rarely used.
  6. Ask for help early. A short email to your instructor on day two of a problem is far better than a long one on day nine.

What good online class help looks like

If you’re looking for the best online class help, look for something that explains. A good tutor shows you why a step works, gives you a similar problem to try on your own, and leaves the graded work to you. They don’t log into your account, complete your assignments, or promise a grade. That’s how we work, and it’s the only kind of help that still helps you on the exam.
