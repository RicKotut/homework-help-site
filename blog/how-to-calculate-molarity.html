How to Calculate Molarity Step by Step

Part of the College Science Survival Guide

Molarity shows up constantly in intro chemistry, and the calculation itself is simple once the formula is memorized — most chemistry homework help requests on this topic come from mixing up units, not from the math itself.

The molarity formula

Molarity (M) = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution

Units matter here: if you're given milliliters, convert to liters first (divide by 1000). If you're given grams of solute, convert to moles first using molar mass.

Step-by-step method

  1. Convert the amount of solute to moles, if it isn't already given in moles.
  2. Convert the volume of solution to liters, if it isn't already given in liters.
  3. Divide moles of solute by liters of solution.

Molarity calculation example

What is the molarity of a solution made by dissolving 20 g of NaOH (molar mass 40 g/mol) in enough water to make 500 mL of solution?

Moles of NaOH: 20 g ÷ 40 g/mol = 0.5 mol

Convert 500 mL to liters: 500 ÷ 1000 = 0.5 L

Molarity = 0.5 mol ÷ 0.5 L = 1.0 M

Dilution problems: a common variation

Chemistry concentration problems often ask you to dilute a solution to a new concentration, using: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ (initial molarity × initial volume = final molarity × final volume). Plug in the three values you know and solve for the fourth.

Common mistakes

Forgetting to convert mL to L before dividing. Molarity is always moles per liter — using milliliters directly gives an answer 1000 times too large.

Using the mass of solute instead of moles. Molarity is based on moles, not grams — always convert grams to moles using molar mass first.
