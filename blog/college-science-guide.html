College Science

The College Science Survival Guide

A guide covering intro Chemistry and Biology — and where to go for help on each tricky topic.

Intro college science courses ask you to do two different things at once: understand a concept, and then apply it through a calculation or a write-up with a very specific expected format. Most of the struggle isn't the underlying idea — it's the mechanics of the calculation, or not knowing exactly what a lab report is supposed to contain.

This guide covers the handful of skills that come up constantly in intro Chemistry and Biology, with a step-by-step walkthrough for each.

The core chemistry skills

  • Stoichiometry — converting between grams, moles, and molecules using a balanced equation.
  • Limiting reactants — figuring out which reactant runs out first and caps how much product you can make.
  • Molarity and concentration — describing how much solute is dissolved in a solution, and calculating it correctly.

The core biology skills

  • Studying efficiently for a content-heavy course where memorization and application both matter.
  • Writing a proper lab report — a specific format that's usually never explicitly taught, just expected.

How to actually study for intro science

Chemistry calculations reward practicing the method until it's automatic — the same few steps, over and over, on different numbers. Biology rewards active recall over re-reading: closing the book and explaining a process out loud or on blank paper, rather than just recognizing it on the page.
