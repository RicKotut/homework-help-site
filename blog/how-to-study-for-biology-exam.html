How to Study for a College Biology Exam in One Week

Part of the College Science Survival Guide

Biology exam preparation is a different challenge than studying for math or chemistry — there's genuinely more to memorize, and re-reading a textbook chapter feels productive without actually building the recall you need under exam conditions. Here's a college biology study guide built around active recall instead of passive review.

Why re-reading doesn't work for biology

Recognizing a term when you see it in your notes is a much easier task than recalling and explaining it cold on an exam. If you want to know how to memorize biology content so it actually sticks, the fix is testing yourself without looking, not highlighting the textbook a fourth time.

A one-week biology exam study plan

  • Day 1–2: Build a master list. Go through your notes and syllabus and list every term, process, and structure you're responsible for — this becomes your self-quiz material.
  • Day 3–4: Self-quiz without notes. Cover your notes and try to explain each item on your list out loud or on blank paper. Mark what you got wrong or fumbled.
  • Day 5: Focus only on your weak list from Days 3–4 — don't re-drill everything equally, concentrate on what you actually don't know yet.
  • Day 6: Do a full practice test or past exam under timed conditions if one is available.
  • Day 7: Light review only — skim your weak list once more, and prioritize sleep over last-minute cramming.

Techniques that actually help

  • Diagrams from memory — redraw a process (like the Krebs cycle or protein synthesis) from a blank page, then check it against your notes.
  • Explain it to someone else (or out loud to yourself) — if you can't explain a process simply, you don't know it yet, no matter how familiar it looks on the page.
  • Group related terms together rather than studying an alphabetical glossary — biology concepts build on each other, and studying them in logical clusters mirrors how you'll need to use them on the exam.
