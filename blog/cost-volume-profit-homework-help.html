Cost-Volume-Profit Homework Help: Break-Even and Target Profit

Use contribution margin to solve break-even and target-profit problems.

Cost-volume-profit (CVP) homework connects selling price, variable cost, sales volume, and operating income. The reliable starting point is contribution margin: the amount each unit contributes toward fixed costs and then profit. This guide shows how to organize the formulas, solve a break-even question, and check a target-profit answer.

Start with contribution margin

Contribution margin is sales revenue minus variable costs. Per unit, it is selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit. The contribution margin ratio is contribution margin divided by sales; it shows the share of each sales dollar available for fixed costs and profit.

CVP formulas

Unit contribution margin = selling price per unit − variable cost per unit

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ unit contribution margin

Units for target profit = (fixed costs + target operating profit) ÷ unit contribution margin

Worked example: break-even and target profit

Assume one product and constant unit costs

A company sells each unit for $50, has variable cost of $30 per unit, and fixed costs of $60,000.

  1. Unit contribution margin = $50 − $30 = $20.
  2. Break-even volume = $60,000 ÷ $20 = 3,000 units.
  3. For a $20,000 target operating profit: ($60,000 + $20,000) ÷ $20 = 4,000 units.

Check: 4,000 units produce $200,000 sales and $120,000 variable costs, leaving $80,000 contribution margin. After $60,000 fixed costs, operating profit is $20,000.

Common CVP assignment mistakes

  • Using total contribution margin where the formula requires contribution margin per unit.
  • Forgetting to add target operating profit to fixed costs when solving target volume.
  • Confusing operating profit with net income after interest and taxes.
  • Rounding the contribution margin ratio too early when solving in sales dollars.

Check the assumptions in the prompt

Basic CVP models assume a stable selling price and variable cost per unit over the relevant range, and fixed costs remain constant within that range. Multi-product problems also depend on the assumed sales mix. State the assumptions your course gives and use the requested units or sales dollars.
