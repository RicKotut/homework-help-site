One-Way and Two-Way ANOVA, Step by Step

Part of the Statistics Software and Methods Guide

Use a one-way ANOVA (analysis of variance) when you want to compare the means of three or more independent groups on one measured outcome. It exists because running several t-tests instead inflates your chance of a false positive. The calculation looks long, but it is the same few steps every time.

The hypotheses

The null hypothesis says all the group means are equal. The alternate hypothesis says at least one group mean is different. It does not say that all of them differ, and that distinction matters when you write your conclusion.

Step-by-step method

  1. State the null and alternate hypotheses and choose your alpha value, usually 0.05.
  2. Check the assumptions: independent observations, roughly normal data within each group, and similar spread across groups.
  3. Find each group mean and the grand mean, which is the mean of all observations together.
  4. Sum of squares between groups = Σ n × (group mean − grand mean)². Sum of squares within groups = Σ (each value − its own group mean)².
  5. Degrees of freedom: between = k − 1 (k is the number of groups), within = N − k (N is the total number of observations). Each mean square is its sum of squares ÷ its degrees of freedom.
  6. F = mean square between ÷ mean square within.
  7. Decide. If the p-value is below alpha (or F exceeds the critical value), reject the null hypothesis.
  8. If it’s significant, run a post hoc test such as Tukey’s to see which groups differ.

Worked example

Exam scores for three teaching methods, five students each. A: 78, 82, 80, 85, 75. B: 84, 88, 86, 90, 82. C: 70, 74, 72, 76, 68.

Group means: A = 80, B = 86, C = 72. Grand mean = 79.33.

Between: 5 × [(80 − 79.33)² + (86 − 79.33)² + (72 − 79.33)²] = 493.33, with 2 degrees of freedom, so the mean square is 246.67.

Within: 58 + 40 + 40 = 138, with 12 degrees of freedom, so the mean square is 11.5.

F = 246.67 ÷ 11.5 = 21.45. The critical value of F(2, 12) at 0.05 is 3.89, and the p-value is about 0.0001. Reject the null hypothesis.

Tukey comparisons: A vs B differ by 6 (p = 0.04), A vs C by 8 (p = 0.007), and B vs C by 14 (p < 0.001). Every pair differs at the 0.05 level.

Write-up: average exam scores differed across teaching methods, F(2, 12) = 21.45, p < .001.

Two-way ANOVA

A two-way ANOVA has two factors, for example teaching method and class time (morning or evening). It tests three things: the main effect of the first factor, the main effect of the second, and the interaction, which asks whether the effect of one factor depends on the other. Read the interaction first. If it’s significant, the main effects can mislead, because the story differs from one condition to the next. Software does the arithmetic, so your job is choosing the factors correctly and interpreting each F and p-value.

Common mistakes

Concluding that all the groups differ. A significant ANOVA only tells you at least one mean is different. Use a post hoc test to find out which.

Stopping at the F statistic. Report the degrees of freedom and the p-value too, in the form F(2, 12) = 21.45, p < .001.

Using a one-way ANOVA on the same people measured several times. Repeated measurements on the same participants need a repeated-measures design.
