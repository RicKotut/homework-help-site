Cell Membrane Transport Homework Help: Diffusion, Osmosis, and Active Transport

Distinguish transport mechanisms by direction, membrane proteins, and energy.

Cell membrane transport questions become easier when you track three things: what is moving, which way it moves relative to its gradient, and whether energy is required. This guide compares diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, and active transport, then applies the distinctions to a typical homework scenario.

Passive transport moves down a gradient

In simple diffusion, particles move across the membrane down their concentration gradient. Facilitated diffusion also moves down a gradient, but a membrane channel or carrier helps a substance cross. Because the movement is passive, it does not directly require cellular energy.

Osmosis is water movement

Osmosis describes water moving across a selectively permeable membrane. For tonicity questions, compare effective solute concentrations on the two sides and identify which solutes can cross the membrane. Water movement depends on that context; do not assume every dissolved particle contributes equally if the prompt distinguishes permeable from nonpermeable solutes.

Active transport can move against a gradient

Active transport uses energy to move substances against a concentration or electrochemical gradient, often through membrane proteins. Check whether a problem describes a pump, energy input, or movement against the stated gradient.

Work through the clues

A solute is more concentrated outside a cell, and a channel allows it to cross the membrane. If the solute has no other force acting on it, net movement is expected down its concentration gradient through facilitated diffusion. If a transporter instead moves the solute from lower to higher concentration, the process requires energy and is classified as active transport.

How to answer transport questions clearly

  1. Name the substance moving: water, an ion, or another solute.
  2. Compare its concentration or electrochemical conditions across the membrane.
  3. Look for a channel, carrier, or pump in the prompt.
  4. State whether movement is passive or energy-requiring and justify it with the gradient.

For osmosis, also describe the relative tonicity and predicted water movement using the definitions from your course.
